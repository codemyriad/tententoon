ten·ten·toon /ˌtɛn.tɛnˈtoːn/ noun

1. a self-repeating image whose copies spiral as they shrink, in the manner of M. C. Escher's Print Gallery.

• a tententoon of the old gallery, winding inward without end.

Origin coined from the Dutch Prentententoonstelling (“print exhibition”): prenten, prints + tentoonstelling, exhibition. The coined word sits right where the two halves meet.

From a picture inside itself to a spiral

Start with a picture that has a clear opening inside it — here, a round portal framed by a painted celestial, clockwork, and botanical wreath sitting on a sunlit workbench against square sage tiles. Place a shrunken copy of the whole workshop inside that circular portal, then another inside that copy's portal, and you get the classic Droste effect (panel 2). Give each copy a slight turn as it shrinks so the painted wreath connects smoothly from one level to the next, and the concentric rings fuse into a single continuous nautilus spiral — a tententoon (panel 3).

Watch the math, then touch it

M. C. Escher drew the first spiral of this kind by hand in 1956 for his lithograph Print Gallery, leaving a small white patch in the center where the lines grew too tight for his pen. In 2003, mathematicians Bart de Smit and Hendrik Lenstra found the exact complex function behind Escher's curved grid and used it to complete the missing center.

Why does complex arithmetic warp a photograph so dramatically without smearing its details, and how does unrolling a circle into a flat strip make the spiral easy to build? Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown) tells the whole story far better than static text can. Watch his video for the explanation, then use the two widgets below to feel every step with your own hands.

1 · How complex functions warp a picture

Treat the picture as the complex plane: every pixel is a single number z = x + iy, and a function f(z) moves every pixel at once. Click any function in the middle column to watch the plane morph from z to f(z), or drag the orange point on the left plane.

Scale & spin Click f(z) = 2z to double every distance from the center, then f(z) = iz to rotate the plane by 90°. Multiplying by a complex number scales and rotates in one move.

Watch the little square Turn on photo, pick f(z) = z2, and drag the point around. Even while the whole workshop bends, the small orange square around your point stays a square — locally it only scales and turns, which is why the sun face, clockwork gears, and basil leaves stay crisp instead of sheared.

Roll & unroll Click exp(z): upright vertical lines wrap around the origin into concentric circles. Then click log(z): it cuts those circles open and unrolls them back into a flat vertical strip.

2 · Unroll, tilt, and roll back up

Now chain those three moves together on the workshop picture. Because the logarithm stretches the region right around the portal, the ornate celestial & botanical wreath ends up filling most of the canvas: log unrolls the round wreath into tall vertical friezes of suns, moons, gears, and vines (panel 1), a complex multiply tilts those friezes so the top of one lines up with the bottom of the next (panel 2), and exp rolls the tilted friezes back into a single continuous spiral (panel 3).

Drag panel 1 (flat strip): ↔ zooms the round views, ↕ rotates them

In short: flatten the nested rings with a logarithm into a repeating strip, tilt the strip to the exact angle where neighboring copies line up, and roll it back up with an exponential.

1 · Drag the unrolled wreath In panel 1, log(z − c) cuts each circular wreath open into a vertical frieze: golden sun at the top, stars, brass gears, and botanical vines along the sides, and the red compass star at the bottom. Grab panel 1 and drag: sliding sideways zooms the round pictures; sliding up and down turns them.

2 · Tilt until the seam locks In panel 2, drag the lean slider away from 12.1° and watch the spiral wreath in panel 3 break along a sharp seam. At β = arctan(ln S / 2π) ≈ 12.1°, climbing one full turn (2π) also steps one copy across (ln S), so each painted frieze feeds directly into the next. Hit snap to lock it.

3 · Unroll and re-roll the spiral In panel 3, pull the flat ↔ spiral slider left and right to watch exp curl the tilted friezes directly into the celestial & botanical nautilus spiral.
